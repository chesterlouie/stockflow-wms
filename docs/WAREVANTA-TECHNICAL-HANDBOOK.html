2. Product overview

Warevanta is a tenant-isolated warehouse management system with a separate Store Operations portal. It covers item and location masters, receiving, putaway, inventory controls, replenishment, counts, outbound fulfillment, returns, traceability, reporting, access administration, and requesting-store transactions.

Warehouse workspace

Company and warehouse users administer masters and execute inbound, inventory, outbound, approval, reporting, and integration work within assigned warehouse scopes.

Store Operations workspace

Assigned Store users request and receive stock, check availability, count stock, scan locations and items, transfer or return inventory, issue stock, print labels, receive decisions, and track fulfillment.

Core design principles

  • Post inventory movements to immutable ledgers; do not directly overwrite balances.
  • Normalize quantities to the item base UOM while retaining entered UOM evidence.
  • Require exact item/location scan evidence at critical mobile transaction points.
  • Keep Store and warehouse approvals separate from execution.
  • Explode virtual kits into physical components for stock, picking, receiving, and returns while retaining the customer-facing parent kit.
  • Enforce company and warehouse scope in both application queries and PostgreSQL row-level security.

4. Security and access

Identity controls

  • Costed bcrypt password hashes; no plaintext password storage.
  • Random, HTTP-only, revocable sessions.
  • Temporary credentials require first-login password change.
  • Email verification, reset tokens, MFA, sign-in throttling, and session invalidation after credential or role changes.
  • Separate browser profiles or private windows are required when testing different users concurrently. Tabs in one browser share cookies and therefore share the latest signed-in session.

5. Master data

Items

Item master supports standard items, spare parts, virtual kits, and stocked kits. Records include SKU, description, base UOM, allocation method, status, category, costing, receiving rules, barcode identities, alternate UOM conversions, components, and item relationships.

Barcodes and UOMs

  • Each barcode belongs to an item and a specific UOM/base-quantity conversion.
  • EA, CASE, pack, pallet, or other active units may each have their own barcode.
  • A receiving or picking scan is valid when the barcode resolves to the expected item and permitted UOM context; receiving quantity being expressed in EA does not invalidate a registered EA barcode.
  • Entered quantity/UOM is retained while operational demand is normalized to base units.
  • Labels are available from item barcode records and relevant receipt/shipment pages.

Warehouses and locations

Warehouses are timezone-aware and access-scoped. Locations have code, type, active status, capacity controls, and printable QR/barcode labels. Operational types include receiving, storage, picking, packing, shipping, hold, and damaged/quarantine.

Warehouse labels: /app/setup/locations/labels. Store locator labels: /store-portal/locators/labels.

Other masters

  • Categories organize item records.
  • Supplier master supports active/inactive status and purchasing references.
  • Requesting Store master includes code, name, status, and default fulfilling warehouse.
  • User access profiles bind people to approved warehouses or one Store profile.

6. Inbound operations

Purchase orders and imports

Create purchase orders, add lines, release demand, or import controlled CSV data. Supplier and warehouse references are validated. Document sequences can generate transaction references automatically.

Dock and gate

Dock scheduling prevents conflicting appointments. Mobile gate check-in records supplier arrival and supports receiving readiness.

Receiving

  1. Select or scan an expected receipt.
  2. Scan the registered item barcode or enter the exact SKU.
  3. Classify quantities as accepted, held, damaged, or short as applicable.
  4. Capture lot and expiry when item rules require them.
  5. Post accepted inventory to the receiving location through the ledger.
  6. Create guided putaway tasks; held and damaged stock stays controlled.

Putaway and cross-docking

Putaway validates source location, destination, item, lot, expiry, and capacity. Cross-docking can route eligible inbound stock directly toward outbound demand while preserving residual putaway quantity and scan evidence.

7. Inventory operations

  • Availability: on hand, reserved, available-to-promise, status, lot, expiry, warehouse, and location.
  • Transfers: atomic paired ledger entries; negative stock is prohibited.
  • Adjustments: controlled reason codes and audit evidence.
  • Status control: available, hold, damaged, expired, and override reasons.
  • Replenishment: rules, generated work, completion, and forecasting recommendations.
  • Counts: cycle/physical/wall-to-wall plans, blind entry, recounts, manager approval, stale-count protection, and variance posting.
  • Traceability: lot, expiry, serial, transaction group, reference type, reference ID, user, and timestamp.
  • Returns: receipt, inspection/disposition, reversal control, and audit trail.

Inventory integrity invariants

  • Reductions cannot exceed available stock.
  • Reservations reduce ATP but do not change on-hand.
  • Movement pairs share a transaction group.
  • Lot and expiry identity is preserved across movement.
  • Serial lifecycle transitions are enforced by database triggers.
  • Store inventory is a separate ledger and changes only after applicable approval.

8. Order management and fulfillment

  1. Create a warehouse/customer order or receive an approved Store request.
  2. For Store requests: Store Operator submits, a different Store Manager approves, then authorized warehouse management accepts or rejects.
  3. Allocate exact inventory using FIFO/FEFO/LIFO rules as configured; approved substitutes may satisfy demand.
  4. Create component-level pick tasks for VKITs and item-level tasks for ordinary items.
  5. Mobile picker scans source location and the actual allocated SKU/barcode.
  6. Exceptions record short pick reasons and may hold or reallocate work.
  7. Packing validates packing location, actual item, and carton.
  8. Seal cartons, print carton labels, and confirm dispatch with carrier/tracking reference.
  9. Dispatch posts inventory issue and makes the shipment available to the destination Store.
  10. Create and close carrier manifests for documented handover.

Order statuses

new allocated picking picked packing packed dispatched plus controlled held and cancelled.

10. VKITs, spare parts, and item relationships

Virtual kits

A virtual kit is a commercial parent item whose inventory remains in required components. The kit configuration records component item, quantity per kit, UOM, optional flag, and active status. Buildable availability is the floor of the lowest required component capacity.

  • Store requests use the parent kit and KIT UOM.
  • Allocation explodes demand into required components.
  • Picking and scanning use physical components or the actual approved substitute.
  • Shipment and Store receipt post components while retaining parent order context.
  • Store availability derives complete kits from Store component balances.
  • VKIT returns require all component scans and preserve parent kit quantity.
  • Warehouse receipt routes returned components to quarantine; reversals restore Store components atomically.

Partial fulfillment and backorders

Partial release is explicit. Warevanta releases only complete, buildable kits; zero-kit release is blocked. The remainder becomes a linked backorder with a unique sequence.

StatusMeaning
WaitingAt least one required component is insufficient.
ReadyAll outstanding complete kits are buildable; authorized warehouse users are notified.
ReleasedAllocation completed and pick work exists.
FulfilledThe linked backorder was dispatched.
CancelledManager cancelled before allocation with mandatory reason and Store notification.

The exception center at /app/orders/backorders shows aging, overdue commitments, component required/ATP/shortage quantities, suggested substitutes, and audited rescheduling.

Relationships

Supported relationships include one-way substitute, reciprocal substitute, superseded-by chain, and compatibility. They support priority, conversion ratio, effective dates, notes, and optional pre-use approval. Spare parts use the normal inventory ledger and may participate in these relationships or serve as VKIT components.

11. Approvals, exceptions, and notifications

  • Configurable approval rules, decisions, delegations, escalation, and notification delivery.
  • Store request, Store receipt, and Store transaction approvals use assigned Store Manager authority.
  • Warehouse Store-request review requires owner/admin/manager plus warehouse scope.
  • Substitution may require explicit approval before allocation.
  • Reversals and destructive corrections use controlled authority and audit records.
  • Warehouse and Store notification tables use unique event keys to prevent duplicates.
  • Notification failure must not roll back the primary Store request transaction.

Operational exceptions include insufficient stock, barcode mismatch, location mismatch, short pick, stale count, invalid workflow state, unavailable Store/warehouse, missing packing location, incomplete VKIT, and approval segregation failure.

12. Reporting, automation, and integrations

  • Standard reports: stock, movement, receiving, fulfillment, expiry, count variance, and valuation.
  • CSV export and scheduled report runs with retry/delivery history.
  • Forecasting rules, stockout projections, replenishment recommendations, and approval.
  • Labor shifts, assignments, task events, and productivity.
  • One-time hashed ERP API credentials with read/write scope and request history.
  • Versioned Item Master, inventory, adjustment, and purchase-order endpoints.
  • Idempotent integration adjustments and negative-stock enforcement.
  • PayMongo subscription webhook and company plan/billing controls.

15. QA handbook

Required test dimensions

  • Role: owner, admin, manager, operator, viewer, Store Manager, Store Operator, Store Viewer.
  • Scope: correct company, wrong company, assigned warehouse, unassigned warehouse, assigned Store, wrong Store.
  • Device: desktop, tablet, narrow phone, rear camera, Bluetooth scanner, manual entry, offline/reconnect.
  • Quantity: zero, negative, decimal, base UOM, alternate UOM, duplicate line, exact stock, shortage, overage.
  • Tracking: no lot, lot, expiry, serial, VKIT components, substitute, supersession.
  • State: first submission, retry, duplicate, stale page, already approved, already cancelled, already dispatched, reversal.

Critical regression scenarios

  1. EA and CASE barcodes both resolve according to Item Master registration and preserve the correct conversion.
  2. Receiving rejects an unrelated barcode and accepts exact SKU fallback.
  3. Store Operator submits a multi-line request; automatic SRQ is unique and duplicate item/UOM lines combine correctly.
  4. Requester cannot self-approve; Store Manager receives and decides the request.
  5. Warehouse users outside the fulfilling warehouse cannot review or allocate it.
  6. Warehouse acceptance releases the request into Order Management.
  7. VKIT availability equals the lowest whole required-component capacity.
  8. Partial VKIT release creates exactly one linked backorder and never releases an incomplete kit.
  9. Component replenishment changes Waiting to Ready and creates one warehouse notification.
  10. Backorder allocation changes Ready to Released; dispatch changes it to Fulfilled.
  11. Cancellation is allowed only before allocation and records reason, actor, time, audit, and Store notification.
  12. Rescheduling preserves original date and records every revision.
  13. Store receipt classification totals expected quantity; only approval posts Store stock.
  14. Store transfer, return, issue, and count enforce location and item scans.
  15. VKIT return expands exact required components; warehouse receipt and reversal remain atomic.
  16. Two users tested concurrently use separate browser profiles, never tabs in one shared session.

16. Deployment and operations

QA release flow

  1. Review git status; preserve unrelated user work.
  2. Run focused tests and production build.
  3. Stage only release files; run git diff --cached --check.
  4. Commit with a functional message and push main.
  5. Render auto-deploys. Hosted startup runs pnpm run db:migrate against configured Neon admin URL before starting the app.
  6. Poll https://warevanta-qa.onrender.com/api/ready until both revision and migration match.
  7. Execute role-based smoke tests and record results.

Environment requirements

Configuration is validated by repository scripts. Keep database admin URL separate from least-privilege application access, use secure session/email/billing secrets, and never commit environment files. The Render free service may sleep after inactivity, adding roughly 50 seconds or more to the first request. This is cold-start behavior, not necessarily application failure.

Local service

.\warevanta-wms.cmd start
.\warevanta-wms.cmd status
.\warevanta-wms.cmd restart
.\warevanta-wms.cmd stop

Local PostgreSQL must be running for local migration/E2E commands. A local connection refusal does not imply Neon failure. Use /api/ready to verify the hosted database.

Backup and rollback

Follow ops/STAGING-RUNBOOK.md. Application rollback and database rollback are separate decisions. Migrations are forward-only by default; design corrective migrations for deployed schema changes. Verify backup freshness and restore procedures before production promotion.

18. Known constraints and enhancement register

  • Render/Neon free-tier cold starts and quotas can affect QA responsiveness; confirm current provider limits before production sizing.
  • Browser sessions are cookie-based; simultaneous multi-role QA needs separate browser profiles.
  • Camera scanning depends on browser permission, HTTPS, device camera quality, lighting, and barcode print quality.
  • Offline scan drafts are device-local; authoritative inventory validation occurs online.
  • The hosted background worker checks backorders every minute while the Render service is awake; a free-tier sleep pauses detection until the service wakes.
  • Module expansion under consideration: Merchandising, broader Order Management, Demand Planning, and Supply Planning. Subscription packaging will be decided separately.
  • Before production: load/performance testing, accessibility audit, disaster-recovery exercise, security review, observability thresholds, data-retention policy, and full role/UAT sign-off.

Release 070 technical addendum

MigrationCapability
070Actionable VKIT backorder component substitution with intent records, conversion/ATP preview, controlled approval routing, preferred allocation, readiness recalculation, state notifications, audit evidence, and actual-substitute mobile scanning.

Data: vkit_backorder_substitution_intents stores one selected relationship per order/component with pending, approved, rejected, applied, or cancelled status. vkit_backorder_shortages exposes relationship ID, ratio, approval requirement, and substitute ATP.

Controls: only owner, administrator, or warehouse-scoped manager may select; order must remain New and the relationship must be active/effective. Required substitute quantity equals component shortage × conversion ratio. Selection is blocked when substitute ATP is insufficient. Approval-required selections enter the existing item-substitution workflow; successful allocation marks the intent Applied. Pick tasks and scans reference the actual substitute SKU while allocation retains demanded component identity.

QA: verify approval-free and controlled relationships, conversion ratios other than one, insufficient ATP, inactive/expired relationships, rejection, readiness recalculation, duplicate selection, allocation preference, actual barcode scan, Store/warehouse notifications, tenant isolation, and attempts after picking starts.

Release 071 technical addendum

MigrationCapability
071Multi-option VKIT component substitution and supersession-chain resolution.

Data: vkit_backorder_substitution_options recursively derives direct, reverse reciprocal, and supersession paths. Paths carry relationship IDs, visited item IDs, cumulative conversion ratio, approval requirement, priority score, depth, required quantity, ATP, sufficiency, and rank. Selected intents retain the target, exact path, depth, and effective ratio.

Controls: traversal is company-scoped, active/effective only, cycle-safe, and capped at eight relationships. Ranking favors sufficient ATP, then fewer hops, lower priority score, and stable SKU/path ordering. The server resolves the submitted target again under row lock. Allocation prioritizes the selected approved target and can fall back through approval-free ranked options.

QA: test multiple direct options, reciprocal reverse use, two- and multi-hop supersession, cumulative non-one ratios, mixed UOM display, expired/inactive edges, cycles, eight-hop limit, insufficient ATP, controlled paths, rejection, stock changes between selection and allocation, actual-item scan enforcement, tenant and warehouse scope, and audit/notification evidence.
